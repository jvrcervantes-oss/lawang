    What we sell

    We don’t sell finished houses. We offer a right over a plot in one of our projects, plus the turnkey construction of a new villa on it. The client makes those two decisions with you, and together they give a price that is fixed in writing before signing.

    Fixed price

    diversity_3
    Why they cost less

    We build with our investors. We all go together, hand in hand.

    Our villas are cheaper because we build them together with the people investing in them: the client isn’t buying a house someone else has already finished and marked up. They come in from the start and build with us. It’s the first argument to give when they ask about price.

    First things first

    The four words you need to master

    The client will ask “who owns the land?” on the first call. This is what each word means. The rule is simple: if the client buys in their own name, it’s Hak Sewa; if they set up their own company (PT PMA), it’s HGB.

    menu_bookSee the full glossaryarrow_downward
    Not offered

    Hak Milik

    Full, permanent ownership of the land. Only an Indonesian citizen can hold it: never a foreigner, and never a company with foreign capital. If anyone offers it to a foreigner, that is illegal.

    Registered land right

    HGB

    Hak Guna Bangunan: the right to build and own buildings on a piece of land. It is held by an Indonesian company with foreign capital (PT PMA). It lasts 30 years, extendable by 20 and renewable for another 30, only if applied for: it is not automatic.

    Lease

    Hak Sewa

    A lease of the land, agreed by contract with its owner. The term, the extensions and what happens to the house at the end are whatever that contract says.

    Market term

    Freehold

    It isn’t a concept in Indonesian law: it’s what the Bali market calls many different things, and the client hears “it’s mine”. Don’t use it yourself. If the client says it, answer with the exact name of what they are buying (Hak Sewa or HGB).

    Step 6 · Red lines

    What you say and what you don’t

    What you tell a client on WhatsApp or on a call can bind the company just like a contract. These rules aren’t about style: they protect the buyer, Lawang and you.

    blockNever say

    • “Hak Milik”, “full ownership”, “in your name forever”.A foreigner cannot hold Hak Milik in Indonesia. Saying it, especially in Bahasa, describes exactly what the law prohibits.
    • Anything that sounds like putting the land in someone else’s name.The land is never put in a third party’s name. If a client suggests it, the answer is no.
    • “Freehold”.In Bali it’s used with many meanings and the client hears ownership. If the client says it, answer with the exact name of the right in their project (Hak Sewa or HGB) and show them the contract.
    • “You’ll make X%”, “it’s sure to go up in value”, “guaranteed returns”.No return figure is guaranteed.
    • Where the money goes, from memory.Not “it goes into an escrow account” and not “you pay it to us”: where each payment goes (the company, the notary, the landowner) is set by the contract for that deal. If you don’t know, ask your manager.
    • “I’ll extend it for you”, “I’ll give you a discount”, “we’ll change the milestones”, “you’ll get it in such-and-such a month”.Your manager decides those, and they go in writing. A promise from you on WhatsApp commits the company.
    • Tax advice about what the client will pay in their own country or in Indonesia.

    check_circleWhat you can say

    • “It depends on how you buy: in your own name it’s Hak Sewa; with your own company, HGB.”And the details of each are written in their contract.
    • “This is the financial example for one specific plot.”The forecast in the investor deck is exactly that: an example, with its assumptions. Not a promised result.
    • “The price is fixed in writing before you sign.”It’s true, and it’s one of the things that reassures people most.
    • “The euro amounts are indicative.”The binding figure is in rupiah (IDR), with the exchange rate and the conversion date set by the contract.
    • “I’ll check and confirm it in writing.”Always better than improvising.
    07
    Step 7 · Objections

    The usual objections

    These questions come up in almost every sale. The model answer is a safe starting point; put it in your own words, but don’t add promises to it.

    “Is it legal for a foreigner to buy in Indonesia?”expand_more
    Model answer
    A foreigner can't hold Hak Milik, full ownership of the land, which is reserved for Indonesian citizens. What we offer is a right the law does allow foreigners to hold: if you buy in your own name, a notarised Hak Sewa lease; if you set up your own foreign-owned company, an HGB right. Your contract sets it out, and we recommend your own lawyer reviews it.

    Don’t go into the details of the legal structure from memory: show them the contract for that project.

    “What happens when the term runs out?”expand_more
    Model answer
    It depends on how you buy. Through your own company it is HGB: it runs for 30 years, extendable by 20 and renewable for another 30, but it has to be applied for and the requirements met. In your own name it is Hak Sewa: the term, the extension and what happens to the house at the end are in your contract, and we go through them with you before you sign.

    Never say that renewal is automatic or guaranteed.

    “What if the build is delayed?”expand_more
    Model answer
    The construction contract sets the timeline and which delays extend it and which don't (force majeure, permits, the client's own decisions or payments). You pay for completed construction milestones, not the full amount upfront, and you can follow progress in your buyer portal.

    Don’t promise dates or penalties that aren’t in that contract.

    “What if you go bust halfway through the build?”expand_more
    Model answer
    You never pay ahead of the build. The villa is paid in stages, and each stage is paid once it is finished and certified. Your right to the plot is signed before a notary before construction starts: a lease (Hak Sewa) in your name, or HGB held by your company. If construction ever stopped, what you've paid for is already built on your plot, and your land right stays in force for the full signed term.

    Never say “the land is yours” or “you’re the owner” to someone buying under Hak Sewa: it’s a lease with a fixed term. And what happens to a half-finished build (who completes it, refunds) is for the contract to say, not you.

    “Can I sell it later?”expand_more
    Model answer
    Yes. You can sell, transfer or rent out your right to the plot and the villa for the remaining term. The transfer conditions are set in your contract, and the notary walks you through the process. If you hold it through your company, the usual route is selling the company or transferring the HGB.

    No “complete freedom”: whoever buys a Hak Sewa takes on the years that are left, and the transfer may need the landowner’s consent. Before you promise anything, check the assignment clause in that contract.

    “How much will I make?”expand_more
    Model answer
    We don't guarantee returns. The example in the investor deck is calculated for one specific plot, with occupancy and nightly-rate assumptions that may not be met. What I can tell you is how it works: you can hand the management over to Betterplace, which charges 20% of what the villa earns, only if it earns, with no monthly fees.

    The example is in the project’s investor deck. Always explain that it’s an example.

    “Why should I pay before I see the house?”expand_more
    Model answer
    Because the villa is built for you, off-plan. You pay a reservation that holds your plot, then you secure the land by signing the contract for your right over the plot before a notary, and you pay the construction in stages, each one once it's finished and certified. The reservation terms, including whether it's refundable, are in the letter you sign. And you can come and see the land.

    Don’t claim the reservation is refundable: the letter governs that. And invite them to visit, which is the best answer.

    “It’s cheaper than Canggu or Uluwatu. What’s the catch?”expand_more
    Model answer
    There's no catch: we build it with you. You come in from the start and we build the villa together, instead of buying one that someone else has already finished and marked up. And it's the west coast: same island, same ocean, quieter. Come and see it.

    Use it without comparing returns with other areas.

    “I need to think about it.”expand_more
    Model answer
    Of course. What's missing for you to decide? I'll prepare exactly that for our next call. Does Thursday work for you?

    Don’t hang up without a date for the next step, and log it in their record.

    General information answers for salespeople: each client’s exact terms are in their contract.

    Glossary

    The words you’ll hear

    For you to understand them, not to explain them to the client yourself: the notary or the lawyer gives the legal detail.

    Day to day

    Investor deck
    The full presentation of a project for someone looking at it as an investment: plot plan, photos and a financial example. You can send it to the client.
    Villa landing page
    The web page for each villa model, with photos, layout, roofs and a price configurator.
    Roof
    The villa’s roof. There are several to choose from and they change the price.
    CRM
    The part of the intranet where you add your clients and log every contact and every next step.
    Contract Generator
    The intranet tool that prepares contracts with current prices and sends them for digital signature.
    Reservation Letter
    The document the client uses to hold a plot before the contract. It has an expiry date.
    Plot Hold
    A plot reservation contract with its payment schedule.
    Construction Contract
    The contract for the turnkey villa, with model, roof, extras and payment milestones.
    Milestones and certification
    The build is paid for in stages (milestones). Each payment falls due once that stage is finished and certified.
    Gross price
    The total sale price your commission is calculated on.
    Commission terms
    The rules for your commission: the percentage (1% referral, 5% salesperson), on the gross price, and when it is generated (once 50% of the land has been paid). They are set out in your collaboration agreement.
    IDR
    Indonesian rupiah, the currency in which the binding figure in the contracts is set.
    Escrow
    An account where a third party holds the money until certain conditions are met. Only mention it if the contract for that deal says so.

    The land

    Hak Milik
    Full, perpetual ownership. For Indonesian citizens only: never in the name of a foreigner or a PT PMA.
    HGB
    Hak Guna Bangunan: the right to build and own buildings on a piece of land. A PT PMA can hold it. 30 years, extendable by 20 and renewable for 30, always on application: it is not automatic.
    Hak Sewa
    A lease of the land by contract with its holder. Term and extensions are whatever the contract says.
    Hak Pakai
    A registered right of use. It is the only direct route for a foreigner with a residence permit (KITAS/KITAP).
    PT PMA
    An Indonesian company with foreign capital. It is the legal vehicle for investing from abroad or holding an HGB.
    Freehold
    A market term, not an Indonesian legal concept. The client hears “it’s mine”. Don’t use it yourself.
    Nominee
    Someone who holds the land in their name on behalf of someone else. Illegal in Indonesia. Lawang doesn’t work that way: the land is never put in a third party’s name.

    Formalities (so you understand them if they come up)

    PPJB
    A binding preliminary sale and purchase agreement. It gives a personal right, but does not transfer title.
    AJB
    The deed that transfers a land right, signed before a PPAT and registered with the BPN. It applies to HGB; for Hak Sewa the document is the notarised lease.
    PPAT
    An official authorised to execute land deeds. Not just any notary.
    BPN
    The national land agency, where the right is registered.
    BPHTB
    Tax on acquiring a land right, paid by the buyer. It doesn’t apply in the same way to Hak Sewa: the notary confirms it.
    PBG
    The building permit in force since 2021.
    IMB
    The old permit, replaced by the PBG. It isn’t used for new builds: don’t say the villa “will have an IMB”.
